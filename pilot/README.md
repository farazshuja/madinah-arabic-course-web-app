# Concept paths pilot

19 concepts, 20 unique source cards, three topics: definiteness, description, possession. This is a selected sample from Books 1 and 3A, not full topic coverage.

Serve the repository with `python -m http.server 8000`, then open `http://localhost:8000/pilot/`.

## Views

- Learning path: recognition, construction/distinction, then interacting rules and qualifications. Every explicit pilot prerequisite appears earlier.
- Topic tree: one primary home per concept, with cross-topic links in the detail pane.
- Recall practice: prompt first; rule, examples and sources appear after revealing the answer. Review answers reset when changing concepts. No mastery scores or scheduling are stored.

Background assumptions are recorded separately: basic gender and number, simple nominal sentences, and common prepositions. Levels are editorial complexity estimates, not measured learner difficulty. Review and refine them before expanding the catalog.

## Editorial decisions

- Combined case-agreement cards `b1-c9-a1c` and `b1-c14-6b` into one shared-rule concept. Both original examples and links remain; the later example is an advanced application.
- Grouped human/non-human classification and its agreement rule as a sequence, not as duplicate statements.
- Assembled an adjective/predicate comparison from two cards. The same source may support more than one concept; the mapping is intentionally many-to-many.
- Kept tanwin qualifications, proper-name definiteness, idafah definiteness, and the special pronunciation of “my” separate.
- Preserved ordinary possessive-idafah scope. Other idafah constructions, fuller definite-noun categories, diptote details, and advanced agreement exceptions are outside this pilot.
- Original card content, IDs, bookmarks and reading progress remain in the existing reader. Grouped rules are editorial summaries; `sourceCards` preserves exact original takeaways.

## Data and maintenance

`concepts.json` is generated by `node continuation/build-concept-pilot.cjs`. Edit the curated definitions in that script, then regenerate. Each concept stores its rule, primary/related topics, difficulty, prerequisites, grouping treatment, editorial note, examples, recall prompt/answer, and all supporting source links. Examples are copied verbatim from the existing cards; synthesized summaries and prompts are separate fields.

Use the pilot to review: topic placement, prerequisite gaps, clarity of rules, useful combinations, and whether prompts require applying the intended distinction. A retained original card is not necessarily a unique concept, and two cards sharing a tag are not necessarily mergeable.
